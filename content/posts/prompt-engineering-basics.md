Most disappointing AI answers come from vague questions. The model is not reading your mind; it is working with exactly what you typed. A little structure fixes most problems.

## The five-part prompt

Use this order whenever the task matters:

1. **Role**: who the AI should act as.
2. **Task**: the one thing you want done.
3. **Context**: what it needs to know about your situation.
4. **Format**: how the answer should look.
5. **Constraints**: length, tone, what to avoid.

### Before and after

A weak prompt:

```
Write something about our new product.
```

A stronger prompt:

```
You are a marketing writer for a small accounting software company.
Write a 100-word announcement for our new invoice-reminder feature.
Our readers are freelancers who hate chasing late payments.
Use a friendly tone, no jargon, and end with one clear call to action.
```

The second version tells the model who it is, who it is writing for, how long to go, and how to sound. The answer is usable on the first try.

## Three habits that help

- **Show an example.** Paste one sample of what good looks like and say "match this style."
- **Ask for steps.** For analysis or planning, add "think through this step by step before answering."
- **Iterate.** Treat the first answer as a draft. Reply with "shorter," "more concrete," or "explain it to a beginner."

## Check the answer

AI can sound confident and still be wrong. For facts, numbers, names and quotes, verify against a reliable source before you publish or act.

## Try it today

Pick one task you do every week, such as an email, a summary or a meeting agenda. Write it as a five-part prompt, save it, and reuse it. That single saved prompt is your first prompt library.
